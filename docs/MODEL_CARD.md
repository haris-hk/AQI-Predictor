# Model Card — Karachi 3-day AQI forecaster

## Intended use

Forecasts the US Air Quality Index for Karachi one, two and three days ahead,
as a daily mean and a daily maximum. Intended for **general public awareness
and personal activity planning** — deciding whether to exercise outdoors,
whether to keep windows shut, whether a sensitive household member should
stay in.

## Out of scope

- **Not a regulatory or official advisory.** It is model output, unaffiliated
  with any environmental authority.
- **Not a clinical tool.** It must not be used to make medical decisions.
- **Not valid outside Karachi.** The models are fitted to one location's
  emissions and meteorology. Applying them elsewhere without refitting will
  produce confident nonsense.
- **Not an hourly forecast.** The targets are daily aggregates.

## Architecture

Six independent regressors, one per (target, horizon) pair. The winning
family is selected per pair by walk-forward cross-validation from a ladder of
baselines, linear models, tree ensembles, SARIMAX and neural models. Gradient
boosting and extremely randomised trees are the usual winners.

## Training data

Open-Meteo CAMS reanalysis and ERA5 weather for 24.8607 N, 67.0011 E. Range,
row counts and known gaps are recorded in `DATA_CARD.md`, generated by the
backfill from what it actually retrieved.

## Evaluation

Expanding-window walk-forward cross-validation with a gap of at least the
forecast horizon between training and validation, then a single evaluation
on the final 90 days, held out chronologically and never used in selection.

Reported per horizon: RMSE, MAE, R², bias, exact AQI-band accuracy,
within-one-band accuracy, and the **skill score against persistence**.

## Promotion rule

A newly trained model replaces the incumbent only if it beats persistence at
every horizon on the holdout **and** is not more than 5% worse than the
incumbent on mean RMSE. Otherwise the incumbent stays and the run records
why. Without this gate an automated retraining loop can only drift downward.

## Limitations and failure modes

| Failure mode | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Dust storms | Sharp AQI spikes with little meteorological warning | Dust and coarse-PM features included; expect underprediction of peaks |
| Regime change (policy, industry, fuel) | Historical relationships stop holding | Daily retraining adapts; the live scoring on the dashboard surfaces drift |
| CAMS versus ground stations | Systematic offset from monitor readings | AQICN cross-check, documented rather than corrected |
| Train/serve weather skew | Live errors exceed backtest errors | Historical-forecast features where available; live scoring measures the gap |
| Extreme values | Error grows in the upper bands | Band accuracy reported alongside RMSE; intervals widen with horizon |

Accuracy degrades with horizon by design and by nature. Day 3 is materially
less certain than day 1, which is why every forecast carries an interval
rather than a bare number.

## Ethical considerations

Air quality information affects health decisions. Two consequences shaped the
design: forecasts are always shown with an uncertainty band rather than as a
precise-looking point value, and the dashboard states plainly that this is
not an official advisory. Underprediction on hazardous days is the costly
error; band-accuracy reporting is what makes it visible instead of averaging
it away into RMSE.
